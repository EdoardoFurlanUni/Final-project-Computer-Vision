Along the final approaches that we decided to adopt, we developed other ideas that we discarded. In this section we briefly summarize some methods that we tried to implement.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Histogram of Gradients + SVM: One of the main problems we found using template matching is its difficulty in dealing with illumination changes and occlusions. For this reason we tried an approach that we studied during the Machine Learning course. This method involves a first part dedicated to training, where features are extracted from test images (in our case we tried the HoG feature extractor) and used to train a SVM classifier. The idea was to use this method to classify each coin
            after the circle detection implemented with the Hough Circle Transform. However, even if we measured a significant improvement in speed, the method never came close to the accuracy of the template matching. This problem may be caused by the fact that the dataset is way too small to give a proper training to the SVM, making it unusable to properly classify images in this specific scenario where data availabilty is very restricted.
    \item Detecting circles: Another approach we explored was to apply thresholding in the CieLAB color space to emphasize gold and copper regions, followed by a morphological opening operation. A major challenge, however, arose with the detection of 2-euro coins, since their silver border was excluded by this threshold. To mitigate this, the resulting mask was dilated and then combined (via a bitwise AND) with a second mask obtained from the HSV color space. In HSV, setting a threshold on color saturation effectively distinguished all coins (not only euros) from the background.
    This method successfully separated the euro coins from the background and from other coins with different colors. However, the morphological operations enlarged the detected regions and caused nearby coins to merge, which in turn prevented the Hough Circle Transform from detecting them accurately.
\end{itemize}
